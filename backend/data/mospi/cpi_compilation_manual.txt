MINISTRY OF STATISTICS AND PROGRAMME IMPLEMENTATION
PRICE STATISTICS DIVISION (PSD)
STANDARD OPERATING PROCEDURE: CONSUMER PRICE INDEX (CPI) COMPILATION & LASPEYRES FORMULA

1. OBJECTIVE AND SCOPE OF THE CONSUMER PRICE INDEX
The Consumer Price Index (CPI) measures temporal changes in the general price level of a representative basket of goods and services consumed by target households. MoSPI releases three distinct indices monthly: CPI (Rural), CPI (Urban), and CPI (Combined). The weighting diagrams are derived from the Household Consumption Expenditure Surveys (HCES) conducted by the National Statistical Office.

2. INDEX CALCULATION: MODIFIED LASPEYRES FORMULA
The All-India and State level CPIs are compiled using the Modified Laspeyres Index Formula. At the elementary item level:
- Elementary Price Relative R_{i,t}: Calculated as the Geometric Mean (GM) of price relatives across selected price quotation markets within each state/centre:
  R_{i,t} = (\prod_{m=1}^{M} (P_{i,m,t} / P_{i,m,0}))^{1/M}
- Sub-group and Group Aggregation: Aggregated using base period expenditure share weights (W_i):
  I_t = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} (R_{i,t} \times W_{i,0})}{\sum_{i=1}^{n} W_{i,0}} \times 100

3. HANDLING OUTLIERS AND SEASONALITY IN PRICES
Field price collectors frequently encounter volatile seasonal spikes (notably in tomatoes, onions, and green vegetables) or missing quotations.
- Truncation and Imputation: If an item quote deviates by more than +/- 3 standard deviations from the district median, the CAPI portal generates an instant audit flag. If an item is temporarily out of season, its price is imputed using the percentage change observed in the nearest available sub-group basket.
- Specifications Drift: When a brand or specification disappears from the urban retail store, price statisticians apply chained quality adjustment rather than direct price substitution to avoid introducing upward inflationary bias.

4. DIFFERENCE BETWEEN CPI AND GDP DEFLATOR
Statisticians must distinguish the CPI from the GDP Implicit Price Deflator:
a) Scope: CPI reflects solely goods and services purchased by domestic consumers, whereas the GDP deflator measures prices of all domestic production (including capital equipment, government services, and exports).
b) Imported Goods: Increases in the prices of imported crude petroleum or foreign consumer tech affect the CPI immediately through the fuel/goods basket, but do not directly enter the GDP deflator (as imports are deducted from GDP).
c) Weighting: CPI uses fixed base-year quantity weights (Laspeyres), which tends to overstate inflation due to substitution bias, whereas the GDP deflator uses changing current-period production quantities (Paasche).
